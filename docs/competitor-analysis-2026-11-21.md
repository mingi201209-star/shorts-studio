First cut within ~2 s for every channel; the top videos cut sooner (1.5 s vs 2.0 s; 1.3 s vs 2.3 s). The first frame always shows something
strange happening, never a title card. Each channel keeps one fixed visual style. Captions: ~55 text changes a minute (Zack D.).

## Common success factors
1. One question per video, and the video ends the moment it is answered (a 31 s video watched 1.4 times beats a 60 s one abandoned).
2. The first frame is the strangest moment, already happening; first cut by ~1.5 s.
3. Topics: common but misunderstood things people see every day (stitches, a McFlurry spoon, a key fob), plus visceral or unsettling
   visuals that get comments.
4. Fast but clear: a new shot every ~2.5 s, narration ~170-210 wpm, word-synced captions; nothing else on screen.
5. A style you recognise in one frame, fixed across every video.
6. Packaging: the title is the hook (a few words, exactly one emoji); descriptions and tags barely matter ("short-form is 99 % content").
7. Volume and patience: many uploads ("lottery tickets"); Jake: Shorts take about a month to reach their first million views and keep
   pulling for a year, so a 1-hour reading is not a verdict.
8. Visuals that work without language (Jake's audience: Indonesia, US, Brazil, India, Vietnam).

## Rules from here
- First cut by 1.5 s: open on the money shot, cut to a second angle of the same moment.
- ~30 s, one question, 170-195 wpm, end on the answer and loop.
- Topics: everyday car and road mysteries people see but cannot explain (overlap check still applies).
- EP.13's look (golden hour, red car, depth of field, sound design) is the house style.
- Titles: a few words, exactly one emoji.
- Judge an upload at 48 h and 7 days, not in the first hour.

Caveat: subscriber, view and revenue figures for these channels are third-party estimates unless marked as from YouTube or the creator;
