# EP.04 — text for the video description (nothing of this is burned into the picture)

The film shows no words except the narration captions (owner decision). What would otherwise be on-screen disclosure lives here.

**Suggested description lines**
- This is a computer simulation, not a crash test: a simplified one-dimensional crash model (1,400 kg car, 50 km/h into a rigid wall) drives a 3D car model. It is not calibrated to any real vehicle or test.
- The crash is shown in extreme slow motion (the whole event lasts under a tenth of a second in the simulation); the run-up and the bounce are played at faster speeds. Both cars use the same crash time scale.
- The blue car is the rigid-front counterfactual: same mass, speed and wall, crush distance of a few centimetres. The silver car is the crumple-zone car.
- The simulated rebound of the rigid car is part of the model.
- The see-through view, the folding crush rails and the driver figure are explanatory 3D objects, not a measured structure of a real car. The rails are drawn as a simple accordion; the real structure of a real car differs.
- The driver is a crash-test-dummy style figure. Its forward travel and chest load come from a simple one-dimensional belt and steering-wheel model fed with the simulated crash pulse (no airbag, no pretensioner, no neck model). The colour goes from green to red with the peak chest load (60 g and above is shown as severe, the limit used in car-safety rules for the chest). It shows the difference between a slow and an abrupt stop; it is not a prediction of real injuries.

**Facts behind each claim in the narration** (all from the simulation, see `crumple_gates.energy_and_claims` and the scene-alignment report)
- driver (model): peak chest load about 25 g with the crumple zone (never reaches the steering wheel) vs about 60 g with the rigid front (hits the steering wheel).
- crush stroke: about 0.62 m (crumple) vs about 0.05 m (rigid); stop time about 87 ms vs about 5 ms; the same kinetic energy is absorbed in both cases.
- the passenger-cell region never moves in the model (checked to 1 mm); the nose line never passes the wall (checked to 5 mm).
