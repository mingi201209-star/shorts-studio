Two rules: (1) a big, readable event in the first second (crash, spin, blow-out); (2) dense, fast content (a fact or a number on every
shot) keeps and replays the viewers who stay.

## Format (every episode)
- 38-42 s, 13 short lines at +15 % speaking rate, a new shot every 2-4 s.
- Opening: labelled split (green / red), test conditions, live numbers from the model; the event happens before 1.0 s.
- Middle: the hidden mechanism (detailed parts, cutaways, lab shots) with fact cards; real-world numbers from textbook formulas or
  standard guidance, never invented statistics.
- End: one piece of advice, then a loop back into the first frame.
- Title pattern: "Why ... ?" or "Same X, Only One ..." + emoji. Every Short goes into a playlist named "One Difference".
- Pinned comment on each upload: "Which difference should we test next?" (engagement and ideas).
- The stylised driver (EP.08) everywhere a person appears.

## EP.09 / EP.10 data (2026-11-21)
Viewers who stay watch almost the whole film (110 % and 96 %): the dense middle works. What fails is the first frame: EP.10 lost 76 %
with small cars on a wide road under four overlay boxes. Rule: the first frame is ONE big subject filling the picture, already moving
(like EP.07's spin, Road Signs' posts), at most one short label per half; numbers and readouts come in after 1.5 s.

## EP.12 data (2026-11-21, 58 min): 21.2 % viewed, avg 0:11
The pattern across every upload: openings inside the cabin or on a person (EP.05 23.4 %, EP.12 21.2 %) and small far-away cars
(EP.10 23.7 %) lose about four in five viewers; big exterior events on road hardware or a whole car (Road Signs 69.9 %, ESC spin 58.4 %,
crumple crash 48.0 %) keep half or more. The "big subject" rule picked the wrong subject: a cartoon driver in a grey cabin is not a
hook. New rule: frame 1 is a whole car (or road hardware) outdoors in the middle of a violent, readable event, filling the frame. No
cabin or person openings. Topics return to the hidden engineering of roads and cars (the Road Signs kind), not safety advice.

## EP.15 data (2026-11-21, 81 min): 13.7 % viewed, 173 views, stalled
A frame can satisfy "the event is already happening" and still read as nothing: the head-on car pressing three thin ropes looked
parked on a lawn. Rule added: judge frame 1 as a still image at phone size. It must show motion by itself (debris in the air, parts
breaking or flying, a car at an angle mid-skid, a big deformation), from a side or high three-quarter view where the deformation is
obvious. Head-on and straight-behind views flatten the action. No shot of the subject just driving longer than 1.5 s.
