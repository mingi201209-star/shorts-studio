## Description
Why did a steel wall just jump out of the road? It's a security barrier: in an emergency it rises in about a second. Under the road a hinged steel plate waits in a concrete pit, and hydraulic pistons swing its front edge up nearly three feet. A car that hits it doesn't just bounce off the steel: thick struts push the crash down into tons of buried concrete, so the car stops dead and its back end flips up. Tested ones stop a 15,000-pound truck going 50 mph.

## Claims check
- Wedge (plate) barriers: a steel plate hinged over a shallow foundation pit, raised by hydraulics; emergency fast operation is
  commonly about 1-2 s (model: 1.0 s); raised heights are typically about 2-3 ft (model: 0.9 m = 2.95 ft, "nearly three feet").
- Crash ratings: ASTM F2656 M50 = a 15,000 lb (6,800 kg) medium-duty truck at 50 mph; P1 = it goes at most 1 m past the barrier
  (model truck: 0.85 m). "Tested ones can stop" a truck like that; not every barrier is rated that high.
- The car: 50 mph into a rigid face, nose crush 0.71 m, about 36 g, stops in 0.064 s; the impact below its centre of mass pitches it
  (rear lifts 1.2 m, 17 degrees) and it drops back on its wheels (explanation model, not a test result).
