## Description
What stops a giant ship from knocking down a bridge? Round islands called dolphins: steel rings packed with up to 6,700 tons of crushed rock. When a ship hits one, the ring bends, the rock packs tighter and the ship's bow crumples, so the ship stops before it ever touches the pier. Florida built them after a ship knocked down its Sunshine Skyway bridge in 1980.

## Claims check
- Dolphins are independent structures placed to intercept a ship before it reaches the pier; they deflect the vessel and absorb the
  collision energy, mostly by plastic deformation of the ship's bow and the dolphin (vessel-collision design literature and the
  AASHTO guide that grew out of the 1980 Skyway collapse; e.g. https://www.structuremag.org/article/bridge-safety-in-the-spotlight-after-baltimore-collapse/,
  Wikipedia "Dolphin (structure)").
- The replacement Sunshine Skyway (opened 1987) was planned with 36 dolphins; each was to hold up to 6,700 tons of crushed rock and
  withstand nearly 30 million pounds of force; PBS: the dolphins around each pier were designed for an 87,000-ton ship
  (https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/buildingbig/wonder/structure/sunshine_skyway.html; WUSF and Yahoo/Tampa Bay Times retrospectives).
  The film says "up to six thousand seven hundred tons" and "nearly thirty million pounds", not a count of dolphins.
- On May 9, 1980 the freighter Summit Venture struck a pier of the old Sunshine Skyway and a span fell (Britannica, WUSF):
  "Florida built them after a ship knocked down its Sunshine Skyway bridge in nineteen eighty".
- The model (benchmarks/ship_dolphin/dolphin_motion.py): an 87,000 t ship at 6 knots (414 MJ) into a 9 m radius ring with 12 m of
  rock to the bed; the push-back grows to at most 125 MN as the bow (60 %) and the ring (40 %) give: it stops in 2.6 s after 4.5 m,
  peak 27.4 million lb, 39.5 m short of the pier footing; the ring holds about 5,900 t of rock (packed at 1.6 t/m3). A simplified
  explanation model, not a vessel-collision analysis; the bridge, the ship and the dolphins' layout are illustrative.
