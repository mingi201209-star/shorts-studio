## Description
How do you stop a jet doing 150 miles an hour? On an aircraft carrier the deck is far too short to brake, so the jet drops a hook and snags one of four steel cables. Below deck, the cable runs around pulleys to a giant hydraulic piston that forces oil through a valve, turning the jet's speed into heat. It stops a 50,000-pound jet in about the length of a football field, even with the pilot at full throttle in case the hook misses. After 125 landings, the cable is thrown away.

## Claims check
- The Mk 7 Mod 3 arresting gear on Nimitz-class carriers stops aircraft up to 50,000 lb at 130 knots (about 150 mph) in about
  340 ft (Gizmodo, "How to Stop a Jet Dead in Its Tracks in Two Seconds", from Navy figures; DVIDS lists 50,000 lb gross weight).
  A football field is 360 ft end line to end line.
- The engine is hydropneumatic. The cross-deck pendant connects to purchase cables reeved over sheaves to a crosshead that drives a
  ram into a cylinder; oil is forced through the constant-runout valve, so every jet stops in the same runout (Navy training
  manual NAVEDTRA 14310, chapter 3, "Mk 7 aircraft recovery equipment").
- Typically four wires about 50 ft apart; pilots aim for the third (Gizmodo).
- Pilots go to full (military) power at touchdown so the jet can fly off again if the hook misses every wire, a "bolter"
  (Wikipedia: Bolter (aeronautics); Arresting gear).
- A cross-deck pendant's operational life is 125 arrestments, after which it is replaced (US Navy SBIR topic N08-136, "Advanced
  Cable for Arresting Aircraft").
- "Two seconds" is often quoted for a trap; at 150 mph a 340 ft stop takes about 3 s in the model, so the film says "about the
  length of a football field" and gives no time.
- The model (benchmarks/carrier_wire/wire_motion.py) is a simplified explanation model, not the Navy's engine data:
  - a 50,000 lb jet engages at 130 knots with 26,000 lbf of thrust;
  - the pull builds over 9 m to the level that stops it in 340 ft: 2.9 s, peak 2.5 g;
  - 62.7 MJ taken by the engine, about 64 MJ in the Mk 7's description.
