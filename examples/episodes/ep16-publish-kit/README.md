## Description
Why do tires explode on the highway? Most blowouts start days earlier, with a tire that's just a little low. Every time it rolls, the soft sidewall bends flat at the bottom, then springs back, about fifteen times every second at 60 mph. All that bending turns into heat, deep inside the rubber, and the heat slowly cooks the bond that holds the steel belts together. Until the tread peels away and the tire bursts in a split second. And tires quietly lose about a pound of air every month.

## Claims check
- 60 mph = 26.8 m/s; the CC0 tyre's radius 0.281 m gives 15.2 turns a second ("fifteen times every second").
- Under-inflation increases sidewall flexing and the heat it generates (hysteresis); heat degrades the rubber-to-steel-belt bond and is
  the main cause of tread/belt separation; under-inflation is the leading cause of tyre failure (NHTSA, tyre makers). Model: 35 psi
  -> 24 psi raises deflection 37 %, running temperature 50 -> 72 C, bond life cut ~20x (illustrative numbers, not measured).
- "About a pound of air a month": tyres commonly lose about 1-2 psi a month by permeation (NHTSA / tyre makers' guidance).
- "Shreds of rubber on the highway": tread that separated from blown tyres (mostly truck tyres; the film shows a car's).
