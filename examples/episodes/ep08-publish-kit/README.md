## Description
Same crash, same seatbelt. Only one car has an airbag.

The belt holds your chest, but your head keeps going, and without an airbag it hits the steering wheel. A crash sensor feels the car slow down in the first few milliseconds and decides in an instant that this is a real crash. It fires a tiny igniter inside the steering wheel, which sets off a gas generator. The gas splits the cover along a hidden seam and fills the folded bag in about thirty milliseconds.

Here is the clever part: the bag has holes in it. As your head sinks in, the gas escapes through the vents, so the bag slows you down gently instead of bouncing you back. That is why you should sit back from the wheel and always wear your belt.

This is a simplified physics model made for explanation, not a real crash test. The head loads and timings come from that model, not from any real car or airbag. The parts are drawn bigger and simpler than real ones so you can see them.
