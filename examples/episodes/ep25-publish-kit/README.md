## Description
Why does this road stand straight up? It's a drawbridge, and Chicago has dozens of them. Each half of the road weighs thousands of tons, yet two motors no stronger than a car's engine lift it in about a minute. The secret is hidden in a pit under the street: a counterweight heavier than the road itself hangs behind the pivot. Like a seesaw, it balances the road almost perfectly, so the motors only have to give it a nudge. As the road tips up, the counterweight swings down into the pit. That's why it's called a bascule, French for seesaw.

## Claims check
- Chicago's movable bridges: sources count 35 operational movable bridges, 44 with 12 out of service, or more than 60 of all types in
  and around the city ("dozens") (Modern Steel Construction, Wikipedia mirror, Medium "Seesaws for giants").
- Michigan Avenue (DuSable) Bridge: each leaf about 4,100 tons (one source 3,400; "thousands of tons"), counterweight about 12,000
  tons ("heavier than the road itself"), two 108 hp motors raise each half in about one minute (Loop North, AISC, Bridgehouse
  Museum, Chicago Loop Bridges). Because it is balanced, only a small amount of energy is needed (Chicago Loop Bridges).
- "Bascule" is French for seesaw; the counterweights balance the leaves (Multnomah County bridge pages).
- The model (a 3,600 t leaf on a 9,000 t counterweight, balanced to 0.3 %, a 60 s opening to 78 degrees with slow starts and stops)
  is a simplified explanation model: the opening needs at most 192 hp, and without the counterweight about 21,000 hp (110 times).
