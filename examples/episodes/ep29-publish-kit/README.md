## Description
Why is this truck holding a giant blade up at the sky? It's carrying a wind turbine blade up a mountain road. Lying flat, a blade this long can't get around these bends, so the trailer tilts it up as steep as sixty degrees. At that angle it sticks out only half as far, and it can swing the blade sideways to clear cliffs and trees. Some of these blades are almost as long as a football field. The truck crawls along at about twelve miles an hour, and if the wind blows over twenty miles an hour the trip stops. Tilting the blade means the mountain roads don't have to be rebuilt.

## Claims check
- The Blade Lifter tilts the blade up to 60 degrees above the horizontal, with a lateral swing of 20 degrees; it moves blades up to
  85 m long on roads with sharp bends; the lifted blade travels only in winds up to 10 m/s (22 mph); tilting avoids changes to the
  road layout (Iberdrola: https://www.iberdrola.com/about-us/our-innovation-model/blade-lifter-wind-turbine-blades-transportation).
- Vendor figure for complicated mountain roads: about 20 km/h (12 mph) (Sinotrailers blade adapter page:
  https://www.sinotrailers.com/?p=804).
- An 85 m blade is 279 ft, almost the 300 ft of a football field between the goal lines; a 118 m blade has been moved this way on
  mountain roads in China (Interesting Engineering: https://interestingengineering.com/videos/how-china-moves-a-118-metre-wind-turbine-blade-up-a-mountain).
- The model (the mountain road of EP.26, the blade at 35 degrees on straights and 60 in bends, swung up to 20 degrees toward the
  road ahead) is a simplified explanation model: lying flat the blade would hang up to 54 m off the road and run into the trees and
  the hillside; tilted and swung it stays at least 18 m above them; at the wind limit the wind's push on the raised blade is about
  40 % of what holds the rig upright, and a gust twice as strong would be enough to tip it.
