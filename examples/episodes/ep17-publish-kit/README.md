## Description
Why is this machine swallowing a concrete wall? It's moving the whole wall over by one lane while traffic keeps flowing. The wall is a chain of concrete blocks pinned together like a bike chain, about 1,500 pounds each. Wheels under the machine grab the T-shaped top of every block, an S-shaped track lifts the chain and slides it sideways twelve feet, and the blocks drop back down in a new line, still locked together. In the morning the busy side gets an extra lane; at night it zips back.

- Moveable barrier systems use about 1 m (3.3 ft) concrete segments with a T-shaped top, hinged together with steel pins; a barrier

  Segments are commonly given at roughly 1,500 lb (model: 725 kg = 1,599 lb from its profile and concrete density).
- One lane = 12 ft (3.66 m) in the US; systems are used for rush-hour lane changes (contraflow) on bridges and highways.
