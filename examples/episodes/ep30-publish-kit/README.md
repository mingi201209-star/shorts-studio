## Description
Why is this giant bridge spinning above a railway? It's a road bridge in China that was built beside the tracks. Trains can't stop for long, so it's built first and turned into place. The whole bridge sits on one giant ball joint in its pier. It weighs forty-six thousand tons and is almost nine hundred feet long. Then it slowly turns on that one joint: it swung fifty-two degrees in sixty-eight minutes, about eight times slower than a clock's minute hand. The railway gave the builders a window of under two hours, and trains were rolling underneath again soon after.

Simplified animation for explanation; the scenery is not the real site.

## Claims check
- A bridge of about 46,000 tonnes and 263.6 m (865 ft) was rotated into place over the Beijing-Guangzhou Railway in Baoding, Hebei,
  on 30 July 2019; it swiveled 52.4 degrees in 68 minutes, using a 110-minute window in the train schedule (China Daily:
  https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/201907/30/WS5d3fdee1a310d83056401c5e.html; The Star:
  https://www.thestar.com.my/news/regional/2019/07/31/rotating-bridge-sets-new-world-record).
- 52.4 degrees in 68 minutes is 0.77 degrees a minute; a clock's minute hand turns 6 degrees a minute, about 7.8 times faster
  ("about eight times slower").
- The spherical hinge (the "ball joint") was reported at 6.5 m across in secondary reports; not confirmed in China Daily, so the film
  only says "one giant ball joint".
- The model (one T-shaped deck balanced on one pier, greased hinge, soft start and stop, deck clear of the overhead wires once
  turned) is a simplified explanation model, not the builders' figures.
