## Description
Same car, same hit from behind at a red light. Only the headrest is different.

The car behind shoves your car forward, and the seat shoves your back. But your head is heavy, and for a moment it stays behind, so your neck bends backward, faster than you could ever react. With the headrest low, nothing stops it: the head whips back over the top, almost sixty degrees in about a tenth of a second. That stretch strains the muscles and ligaments in your neck. That's whiplash.

With the headrest up and close, the head moves less than two inches before the headrest catches it, and the neck stops under twenty degrees.

Set it now: the top of the headrest level with the top of your head, and close, a couple of inches behind your head at most.

This is a simplified physics model made for explanation, not a real crash test: a 10 mph jolt to the struck car, a head on a spring-like neck. Real crashes, seats and people vary.
