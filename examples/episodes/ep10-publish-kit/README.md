## Description
Same speed, same sudden stop. Only the gap is different.

When the car ahead brakes, you don't. Not yet. Your brain needs about one and a half seconds to see it and hit the pedal. At 60 mph that's over 40 yards before you even touch the brake. Then the brakes need room too, and that room grows with the square of your speed: double your speed and you need four times the distance to stop. The car ahead stops in 46 meters. You need 86.

So count three seconds after the car ahead passes a sign. If you reach the sign before you get to three, you're too close. In rain, double it. And a two-second look at your phone at 60 mph is more than 50 yards driven blind. The gap is the only thing that buys you that time.

This is a simplified physics model made for explanation, not a real crash test. It uses a 1.5 s reaction time and hard braking at 0.8 g on a dry road; real reaction times and stopping distances vary with the driver, the car, tires and road.
