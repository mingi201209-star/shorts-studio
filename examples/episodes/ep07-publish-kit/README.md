## Description
Same swerve on a wet road, two cars. One spins out and one doesn't.

Swerve hard and the rear tires can lose their grip first. Once the back starts to slide, the car turns faster than you steer, and steering back is often too late to catch it.

Stability control (ESC) is built to catch that moment. A sensor in the middle of the car measures how fast it is turning, and a sensor on the steering column reads where you are steering. When the car turns faster than the steering asks for, it is starting to spin, so the system brakes just one wheel on the outside front. That one brake drags the nose back out of the spin, using the same valve block and pump as your ABS. It all happens in a fraction of a second. Keep steering where you want to go and let it work.

This is a simplified physics model made for explanation, not a real crash test. Both cars get the same steering; the numbers are not from any real vehicle. Real systems can also brake other wheels and cut engine power; the film shows the main move against a spin. Stability control cannot beat physics: on a slippery road, slow down.
