## Description
Why is this road sinking into the sea? It's a bridge across the Corinth Canal in Greece. When a ship is coming, gates stop the traffic. Then the whole road sinks about twenty-six feet underwater, going down at about thirteen feet a minute, and settles almost on the bottom of the canal. Ships sail right over the top of the drowned road, and with nothing above the water, no mast is ever too tall. A lift bridge would need towers taller than the masts. Once the ship has passed, the road rises again, dripping wet.

Simplified animation for explanation; not an exact model of either bridge.

## Claims check
- Two submersible bridges cross the Corinth Canal, one at each end (Isthmia and Poseidonia); the deck is lowered about 8 m
  (26 ft) below the water so ships can pass over it, with no height limit for masts (Wikipedia, Submersible bridge:
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submersible_bridge; one travel source gives 11 m for Isthmia).
- Sinking speed about 4 m (13 ft) a minute (All Over Greece, Isthmia bridge:
  https://www.allovergreece.com/en/greek-bridges/isthmia-bridge).
- The canal is about 8 m deep and 24.6 m wide at sea level; the bridges are 33 m long and 7 m wide with two lanes (Corinth Canal
  operator: https://corinthcanal.com/the-canal/specifications/?lang=en, https://corinthcanal.com/technical-services/?lang=en).
- The model (a 33 m deck from 1.2 m above the water to 8 m below it at 4 m a minute with soft starts and stops, 2.4 minutes each
  way; a tall ship with a 3.8 m draft and 38 m masts 4.2 m above the drowned road) is a simplified explanation model; a vertical lift
  bridge for the same masts would need towers about 45 m tall.
