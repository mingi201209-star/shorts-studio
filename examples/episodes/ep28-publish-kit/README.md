## Description
Why are hundreds of new cars stacked in this glass tower? It's a car tower next to a factory in Wolfsburg, Germany. New cars roll in from the factory through a tunnel. A robot arm on a central mast picks each one up, turns to face an empty bay, climbs almost seven feet a second, and slides the car in like a book onto a shelf. With no ramps or lanes the cars pack in tight: each tower holds four hundred cars on twenty floors. When a buyer arrives, the robot brings their car down in under two minutes. It's the fastest automated parking system in the world.

Simplified animation for explanation; not the towers' actual machinery.

## Claims check
- Two glass car towers 48 m tall at the Autostadt next to the Volkswagen plant in Wolfsburg, storing up to 800 new cars; about 500
  cars a day are moved for delivery (Autostadt: https://www.autostadt.de/en/explore/car-towers). 400 cars on 20 floors per tower
  (European Traveler: https://www.european-traveler.com/?p=11441).
- New cars arrive from the plant on automated carriers through an underground tunnel system (about 700 m); arms rotate and run
  along a central beam, moving cars in and out of their bays at 2 m/s, about 6.6 ft/s ("almost seven feet a second")
  (Digital Trends; Industry Tap; Amusing Planet: https://www.amusingplanet.com/2012/10/volkswagens-car-towers-at-autostadt-in.html).
- At most 1 min 44 s to bring a car out; Guinness World Records 2014: fastest automatic parking system in the world (Wikipedia,
  Automated parking system: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_parking_system).
- The model (20 floors of 20 bays around a mast, a carriage climbing at 2 m/s with 1 m/s^2, turning at 30 deg/s, a fork reaching
  4.8 m at 0.6 m/s) is a simplified explanation model: the slowest fetch takes 72 s, the red car's 65 s; the silo's footprint is
  about 1.1 m^2 of land per car, against about 25 m^2 per car in a garage with ramps and lanes.
