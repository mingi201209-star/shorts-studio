## Description
Why is the end of this runway built to crumble? Sometimes a jet can't stop and runs off the end. So many airports add a bed of crushable concrete blocks: it looks like pavement, but it's light and full of bubbles. Under the weight of a plane the blocks crush, the tires sink in like driving into deep sand, and every block they crush takes away some of the speed. It's built to stop a jet rolling in at 80 miles an hour (70 knots), and it has already stopped more than two dozen planes with hundreds of people aboard. Afterward, crews just replace the crushed blocks.

## Claims check
- EMAS: crushable cellular-concrete (or foamed-silica) material at the end of a runway; the tyres sink into the lightweight material
  and the aircraft decelerates as it rolls through the bed (FAA EMAS fact sheet; SKYbrary). Products: Runway Safe EMASMAX (cellular
  concrete blocks) and greenEMAS (silica foam).
- Standard design exit speed 70 knots (about 80 mph), minimum 40 knots (FAA AC 150/5220-22A / 22B).
- Arrestments: the FAA fact sheet counts 26 overrunning aircraft stopped with 497 crew and passengers aboard ("more than two dozen
  planes, with hundreds aboard" stays true as the count grows). EMASMAX was reported at 121 runway ends at 71 US airports as of
  September 2024 ("many airports").
- After an arrestment the crushed blocks are replaced.
- The model (65 t, 70 kt, drag rising with how deep the tyres sink in a bed that gets deeper, a little braking) is a simplified
  explanation model: it stops the jet 93 m (306 ft) in at up to 0.75 g; no design figure is claimed from it.
