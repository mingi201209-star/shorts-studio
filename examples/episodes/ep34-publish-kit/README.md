## Description
How do you stop a truck that's lost its brakes? On some steep mountain roads, the escape ramp has steel cable nets hung across it, one after another. Each net is tied to steel tape coiled in boxes inside the walls. When a truck hits, the tape is dragged through pins that bend it back and forth, and bending all that steel soaks up the truck's speed, net after net. They're built to stop a 90,000-lb truck at 90 mph, and a crew can have one ready again in about four hours.

## Claims check
- Mechanical (net) escape ramps on steep mountain roads:
  - Texas's first opened on Loop 375 (Transmountain Road) westbound near I-10 in El Paso on July 1, 2026. It has eight
    energy-absorbing net stations anchored into reinforced concrete walls, is designed to stop a 90,000-pound truck at 90 mph, and
    resets in about four hours after a typical impact (TxDOT newsroom, KVIA, KTSM, TheTrucker.com).
  - Virginia's US 250 ramp at Afton Mountain opened in April 2024: 10 stainless-steel nets for vehicles up to 80,000 lb at up to
    80 mph; the first net is for passenger vehicles, the rest slow a big rig gently enough to avoid injuring the driver (VDOT
    Culpeper District).
  - Wyoming's Teton Pass (WYO 22) arrester has been in service since 2017. Its nets are aircraft cable. The energy absorbers are set
    into the ramp's concrete walls, one tape near the top of the net and one near the bottom on each side, and the tapes are
    replaceable. It is rated 90,000 lb and 90 mph. In July 2025 it stopped a runaway box truck after four nets (WYDOT fact sheet,
    Cowboy State Daily).
- "dragged through pins that bend it back and forth": Metal Bender energy absorbers pull steel tape through offset pins (rollers),
  bending and straightening it; the plastic bending soaks up the vehicle's energy (TTI and TRB Dragnet vehicle arresting system
  reports, 1970s; WYDOT's "metal bender" description).
- The model (benchmarks/truck_net/net_motion.py) is a simplified explanation model, not a product's test data:
  - eight nets 15 m (49 ft) apart, each held by four tapes paying out at 18 kN (about 4,000 lb);
  - an 80,000 lb semi at 60 mph picks up 5 nets and stops in 224 ft, peak about 1.0 g;
  - the 90,000 lb / 90 mph case picks up all 8 nets and stops in 367 ft, peak 1.4 g, with at most 357 ft of tape pulled out
    (each absorber holds 410 ft).
