# Idea Gate run: 8 cross-domain candidates

Ran `shorts_studio idea-gate` against 8 pitches spanning science, city, daily
design (x2), tech, nature, disaster, and history. Results (fail-closed, no
summed score):

| pitch | domain | status | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 ice floats | science | **PASS** (thin) | 5 unique sources vs. 5 required -- zero margin |
| 02 Tokyo G-Cans | city | **PASS** | 7 vs. 6 required |
| 03 foldable umbrella | daily-design | **FAIL** | only 3 unique real sources vs. 5 required -- can't fill the runtime without repeating the same handful of stock photos |
| 04 traffic light order | daily-design | **FAIL** (x2) | first-second visual is a banned generic establishing shot, AND only 3 unique sources vs. 5 required |
| 05 Mariner 1 hyphen | tech | **PASS** | 8 vs. 6 required |
| 06 Carrington Event | nature | **PASS** (thin) | 6 vs. 5 required; pre-photography era means real evidence leans on sketches/newspaper illustrations more than photographs |
| 07 Boston Molasses Flood | disaster | **PASS** (strongest) | 10 vs. 7 required |
| 08 D.B. Cooper | history | **PASS** | 8 vs. 6 required |

Two pitches were rejected outright by the gate exactly as designed: a great
pitch on paper (umbrella) still fails because there simply isn't enough
real, distinct visual material to cover a 40-60s Short without disguised
repeats, and the traffic-light pitch fails on two independent, unrelated
grounds at once (a banned generic-establishing-shot opener *and*
insufficient evidence) -- proving the gate doesn't need every dimension to
fail to reject a weak topic; one fatal defect is enough, and it can catch
more than one at a time.

## Why Boston Molasses Flood (07), not simply "the pitch with no warnings"

Six pitches passed. The Idea Gate deliberately does not rank them -- PASS
means "no fatal defect," not "best." Choosing among PASS candidates is a
production-judgment call, made here on three grounds:

1. **Visual-evidence robustness margin.** 07 clears its requirement by the
   widest margin of any candidate (10 vs. 7 needed, 43% headroom) alongside
   05 (8 vs 6, 33%) and 08 (8 vs 6, 33%). 01 and 06 pass with *zero* and one
   spare source respectively -- in real sourcing, it is common for 1-2
   "candidate" sources to turn out to be duplicates, unusable resolution, or
   AI-generated fakes once actually verified (this happened repeatedly in
   this project's own Titanic and Radium Girls productions). A pitch that
   passes the gate with no slack is a pitch likely to fail for real once
   sourcing starts. 07 has the least of this risk.

2. **Direct structural fit to the engine's own Information Change
   Contract.** The task brief's own worked example for "genuinely new
   information per beat" is, nearly verbatim, this exact story: giant
   storage tank -> tank scale -> rupture point -> chain collapse -> street
   location -> damage record -> period newspaper -> trial result. No other
   candidate maps this cleanly onto 6+ visually distinct, individually
   verifiable information beats.

3. **Strongest, most concrete Ending Payoff.** 08 (D.B. Cooper)'s payoff is
   "and it is still unresolved" -- a legitimate mystery-genre payoff, but an
   intentional anti-climax, which is a weaker fit for "leave a strong
   conclusion, not a summary" than a payoff that is a real, still-in-effect
   institutional change. 02 (Tokyo G-Cans)'s payoff ("it worked, flooding
   dropped 90%") is a good but comparatively unsurprising "the
   infrastructure did its job" ending. 07's payoff -- a wrongful-death
   lawsuit victory in 1919 directly causing the first American requirement
   that engineering drawings carry a licensed professional engineer's
   signature, a regulation still in force today -- is both a genuine
   surprise (nobody expects a candy-adjacent disaster to be the origin of
   modern engineering licensure law) and a real, checkable, ongoing fact,
   not a rephrased summary of the hook.

**Selected: `07_disaster_boston_molasses.json` -- the 1919 Boston Molasses
Flood.**
